# Hackerspace standards: where they came from and why

> Written 30 September 2026. The examples are taken from hackerspaces that
> actually publish their rules, and are cited.

## The short answer to "why bother"

Hackerspace standards did not come from a love of bureaucracy. **They came out of
incidents.**

The safety and property of the people present matter more than an interesting
project. A project can wait; a person cannot. Most of the rules that communities
have actually adopted were written like this: something went wrong, and it turned
out nobody had agreed in advance what should happen.

## The rule that explains all the others

**People's safety outranks the project.**

While that holds, everything else is negotiable. While it does not, there is
nothing to discuss.

## What others already have

### London Hackspace — the most detailed published set

Their rules are public, including the blunt and the funny ones:

- **Rule −1: "Don't be a dick."** Numbered minus one so that it is remembered.
- **Rule Zero: "Do not be on fire."** Fire is the most common cause of a
  hackerspace having to close.
- **"If you see someone working unsafely, it is your duty to stop them and say
  so."** Do not wait until it is too late.
- **"Do not defeat or hack safety features."** For other people's safety as much
  as yours.
- **"If something is broken and you are competent, fix it; do not complain. If
  you cannot fix it, tell someone straight away."**
- **Workbenches are left clear and tools are put back where you found them.**
- **Tools are not taken out of the space.**
- **Guests are the responsibility of the member who brought them. Do not leave
  guests unattended.**
- **Separate policies:** photography, commercial use, internet use.
- **Do not monopolise a tool for days, blocking other members.**

Source: [wiki.london.hackspace.org.uk](https://wiki.london.hackspace.org.uk/view/Rules)

### HackerSpace Mangaluru — code of conduct

Points that are often missing elsewhere:

- Harassment, intimidation and abuse of any kind are not tolerated.
- **Respect other participants' privacy, including the right not to publish
  information without permission.**
- **Obtain explicit consent before recording or sharing any content, including
  photos and video.**

Source: [github.com/HackerspaceMangaluru/code-of-conduct](https://github.com/HackerspaceMangaluru/code-of-conduct)

### PDX Hackerspace — a code with an address to complain to

A code of conduct with a dedicated email address for reports, stating that
reports are handled discreetly and seriously.

Source: [pdxhackerspace.org/code-of-conduct](https://pdxhackerspace.org/code-of-conduct.pdf)

### General practice

- An open wiki as the main carrier of knowledge. **If it is not written down, it
  does not exist.**
- [wiki.hackerspaces.org](https://wiki.hackerspaces.org) — a base of practices
  and theory.

## Why we need this specifically

There are four sources of real harm in a hackerspace, and only one of them is
technical.

**1. Social harm.** Harassment, public humiliation, conflict over space or
equipment. There is nothing to hack about it. The examples above exist precisely
because of this.

**2. Expensive property.** Soldering station, oscilloscope, 3D printer, machine
tools. These are the community's investments, and they break from careless use.

**3. Physical safety.** Electricity, voltage, cutting tools, soldering, powered
equipment. This needs both rules and permission to intervene.

**4. Members' data.** A list with phone numbers, photos, recordings of meetings
— that is personal data, with obligations under the law of Uzbekistan (No.
ZRU-547, see [safety](safety.en.md)). Here the damage is not caused by a hacker
but by an ordinary list nobody thought to delete.

## The standards we are adopting

| Standard | Why | Taken from |
|---|---|---|
| People's safety outranks the project | the base principle | general |
| Do not touch what is not yours without asking | tools and items are shared and expensive | London Hackspace |
| Leave it clean | otherwise only one person can work | London Hackspace |
| Do not block others: one person, one tool | space is limited | London Hackspace |
| Intervene on unsafe work | your safety is your responsibility too | London Hackspace |
| Do not defeat safety features | for everyone, not just you | London Hackspace |
| Consent before filming | people's photos in public are their data | Mangaluru |
| A named address for complaints | otherwise a violation cannot be reported | PDX |
| Anything important goes in the wiki | otherwise knowledge leaves with people | general |
| Members' data follows the law | a duty, not an option | ZRU-547 |

## How standards get agreed

The rule from community practice: **a standard imposed from above does not
work.**

A working order:

1. **The problem is stated as a fact.** Not "I want everyone to be polite" but
   "at the last meeting someone lost a tool because they took it without
   asking".
2. **Discussed in public.** In the chat or at a meeting, in an open issue.
3. **Written short and checkable.** "Do not take what is not yours without asking"
   is checkable. "Be respectful" is not.
4. **Tested in practice.** A month later, look at whether it helped.
5. **Changed if it does not work.** A standard is not carved in stone.

## What we deliberately do not do

- **We do not ban projects that are dangerous by nature.** Drones, high voltage,
  confined spaces — allowed, but under separate rules and with a responsible
  adult present. A ban kills the interest; no rules at all kills the person.
- **We do not require experience to join.** The entry bar is the wish to
  understand something, not a list of projects already done.
- **We do not accumulate paper for its own sake.** Every rule must answer a real
  case that has already happened.
